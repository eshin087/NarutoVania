# Review the rendered sound

Read the sound and audio-QA sections of [combat and audio](../../../docs/chapters/COMBAT_AND_AUDIO.md), [asset ownership](../../../docs/project/ASSET_INDEX.md), and the active audio manifest. Preserve quieter water and existing slider preferences.

Trace the complaint from gameplay action to cue ID, recording, trigger frequency, concurrent sources, and mix. A noisy volley may come from duplicate triggers or one sound per projectile, not from a bad recording. Check attack-start, contact, animation events, and reflected-projectile cues before replacing files.

Where playback is available, audition the isolated cue and the repeated real context: combo, parry streak, barrage, ultimate, pause/retry, and scene transition. Decode/peak/silence analysis supports this; it cannot prove pleasant sound. If listening is unavailable, record that precisely and avoid subjective sign-off.

For authorized replacement, choose reusable recordings with source, author, license, attribution, and edit notes. Prefer a short primary recording and restrained variation. Do not extract a mixed anime/YouTube soundtrack for redistribution merely because it is a reference. Do not solve every cue by layering, pitch shifting, or adding oscillator noise.

Verify priority/ducking, bounded effects per volley, vocal cooldowns, music crossfade ownership, and cleanup on pause/retry/exit. A one-time settings migration must preserve later user choices. Keep visual and audio contacts aligned without duplicate damage/cues.

Record changed cue IDs, paths, provenance, signal measurements, actual listening coverage, and remaining issues. For an audio review request alone, return findings without replacing the mix.
